**Readings**

- Alisha C. Holland (2016), "Forbearance", *American Political Science Review* 110(2)
- Xabier Gainza, Andrés Espejo and Felipe Livert (2025), "The Right and the Politics of Labor Informality Enforcement", *Latin American Politics and Society* 67
- R. Daniel Kelemen and Tommaso Pavone (2023), "Where Have the Guardians Gone? Law Enforcement and the Politics of Supranational Forbearance in the European Union", *World Politics* 75(4)
- Steven Levitsky and Daniel Ziblatt (2018), *How Democracies Die*, ch. 5 and 6

**Where to start:** the brief keeps two ideas apart: Holland's forbearance (not enforcing the law against citizens) and Levitsky and Ziblatt's institutional forbearance (politicians holding back from using legal powers). The long episode takes the four readings in turn and ends on that comparison; the standard one gives the essentials. The first infographic compares who forbears, toward whom and why.

**Notes on the infographics:** infographic 2 has several typos ("roplaced", "inftingements", "Treety", "Barrose", "forbearanee", "aoross", "secrificing"). Its EU example puts the 67% drop in infringements on Barroso alone; Kelemen and Pavone's figure covers 2004–2018, which also includes the Juncker Commission.
