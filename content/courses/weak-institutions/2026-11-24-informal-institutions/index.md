**Readings**

- Gretchen Helmke and Steven Levitsky (2004), "Informal Institutions and Comparative Politics: A Research Agenda", *Perspectives on Politics* 2(4)
- Daniel M. Brinks (2006), "The Rule of (Non)Law: Prosecuting Police Killings in Brazil and Argentina", ch. 10 of Helmke and Levitsky (eds.), *Informal Institutions and Democracy: Lessons from Latin America*
- Anna Persson, Bo Rothstein and Jan Teorell (2013), "Why Anticorruption Reforms Fail: Systemic Corruption as a Collective Action Problem", *Governance* 26(3)
- Akshay Mangla (2015), "Bureaucratic Norms and State Capacity in India: Implementing Primary Education in the Himalayan Region", *Asian Survey* 55(5)

**Where to start:** the long episode works through the typology, Brinks's police killings, corruption as a collective action problem and Mangla's two Himalayan states. The standard episode gives the essentials. The first infographic puts the four types on one map.

**Notes on the infographics:** all three were made from Helmke and Levitsky, Persson et al. and Mangla, before the Brinks chapter was added, so they don't cover it. Infographic 2 misspells "Divergent" as "Divergant", and its Persson et al. line is loosely worded: their point is that reforms assume "principled principals" who are missing where corruption is the norm. Infographic 3 repeats "seen in", and Persson et al.'s interviews were in Kenya *and* Uganda.
